Hi, I'm Ron. I AM what I do.

roniam = Ron + IAM. Identity in both senses: the field I work in, and the fact that developing engineers is who I am.

I'm Ron Bar-Tor, a Senior Engineering Manager whose technical home is identity: OAuth 2.1, OIDC, SAML, WebAuthn, Kerberos, zero trust. Machine learning is the other love, found in my graduate studies and never let go. I built this site to pass on what I've accumulated: five learning paths spanning identity, software engineering, JavaScript, ML, and cloud-native infrastructure from Docker to Istio, plus an engineering blog. I use AI the way a leader uses a team, delegating the mechanical and keeping the judgment (more on that just below). The aim is to develop engineers, not park them in tutorial hell. Every lesson ends with you doing the work, and CI gates hold the material to the same bar I'd hold a team to.

Where AI fits, and where it doesn't

I'll be straightforward: AI generated this site, and it generated large parts of the dojos. That was a deliberate engineering decision, and it's the same call I make as a technical leader every day: know what to delegate, and know what only you can do. The curriculum, the architecture, the design decisions, and every technical judgment in these lessons are mine, drawn from years of hands-on work in identity and platform engineering. When the problem needed my hands, it got them: the grading model, the CI gates, the protocol details a generator would happily get wrong. When the work was mechanical, I delegated it and reviewed the output, exactly as I would with a team. The typing was delegated. The thinking wasn't. And the same five CI gates hold every line to the bar, no matter who or what wrote it.

Proof of work

Don't take my word for it. Take the course.

Tutorial hell is watching someone else code. The way out is doing the work, so every lesson here ends with an exercise you complete yourself, in the page. Five learning paths; the three core dojos compile to a single self-contained HTML file with zero dependencies, so they run offline, from a USB stick if you like, while ML Dojo lives in its own repository and the cloud-native path takes you from Docker to a running Istio mesh. Every push is gated by five CI checks, including one that executes every graded exercise against its own reference solution. Free, no accounts, no tracking. Knowledge shared is the whole point.

Management, practiced

How I lead

Principles I hold myself to as a manager, with the receipts where they can be public.

“People ship software. I ship teams.”

My output is the team's output. 1:1s are about the person, not the status report; growth plans are written, revisited, and real. The best week is one where a decision got made well without me in the room.

“Feedback is delivered fresh, never frozen.”

Hard conversations happen in days, not at review season. Praise in public, correction in private, both specific enough to act on. A surprise in a performance review is a failure, and it is mine.

“Disagree with me in public and win.”

I run decisions so the best argument beats the highest title: written options, explicit trade-offs, reversible-by-default calls, and a record of why. When someone junior overturns my plan with better evidence, that's the system working.

“Encode the bar in gates, not in heroics.”

Quality that depends on someone remembering to be careful doesn't survive a busy quarter. I put the standard into CI where it can say no on my behalf; this site ships behind five such gates, and they've caught what review missed.

“Honest numbers stay useful. Inflated ones stop.”

I'd rather report an uncomfortable 8% truthfully than a comfortable 100% nobody can act on. The repo badge here says content checks, not tests, for exactly that reason: flattered metrics stop informing decisions, including mine.

“Volunteer the weakness before it's found.”

Postmortems are blameless, limitations are stated first, and "here's what I'd do differently" comes from me before anyone asks. Owning the gap is cheaper than defending it, in code review and in careers.
