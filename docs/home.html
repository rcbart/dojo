Hi, I'm Ron. I AM what I do.

roniam = Ron + IAM. Identity in both senses: the field I work in, and the fact that developing engineers is who I am.

I'm Ron Bar-Tor, an engineering manager whose technical home is identity: OAuth 2.1, OIDC, SAML, WebAuthn, Kerberos, zero trust. I built this site to pass on what I've accumulated: three interactive courses spanning identity, software engineering and JavaScript, plus an engineering blog. The aim is to develop engineers, not park them in tutorial hell: every lesson ends with you doing the work, and CI gates hold the material to the same bar I'd hold a team to.

Management, practiced

How I lead

Principles I hold myself to as a manager, with the receipts where they can be public.

“People ship software. I ship teams.”

My output is the team's output. 1:1s are about the person, not the status report; growth plans are written, revisited, and real. The best week is one where a decision got made well without me in the room.

“Feedback is delivered fresh, never frozen.”

Hard conversations happen in days, not at review season. Praise in public, correction in private, both specific enough to act on. A surprise in a performance review is a failure, and it is mine.

“Disagree with me in public and win.”

I run decisions so the best argument beats the highest title: written options, explicit trade-offs, reversible-by-default calls, and a record of why. When someone junior overturns my plan with better evidence, that's the system working.

“Encode the bar in gates, not in heroics.”

Quality that depends on someone remembering to be careful doesn't survive a busy quarter. I put the standard into CI where it can say no on my behalf; this site ships behind five such gates, and they've caught what review missed.

“Honest numbers stay useful. Inflated ones stop.”

I'd rather report an uncomfortable 8% truthfully than a comfortable 100% nobody can act on. The repo badge here says content checks, not tests, for exactly that reason: flattered metrics stop informing decisions, including mine.

“Volunteer the weakness before it's found.”

Postmortems are blameless, limitations are stated first, and "here's what I'd do differently" comes from me before anyone asks. Owning the gap is cheaper than defending it, in code review and in careers.
